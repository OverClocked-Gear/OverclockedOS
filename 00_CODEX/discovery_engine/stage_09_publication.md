# Stage 09: Publication

## Purpose

Generate audience-specific communication.

Publication creates audience-facing outputs from applications and canonical knowledge. Every publication becomes a new observation that can re-enter the scientific lifecycle.

## Examples

- Book chapters
- LinkedIn articles
- White papers
- Presentations
- Discussion prompts
- Course modules
- Podcasts
- Case studies

## Outputs

- Publication-ready deliverables
- Audience-specific variants
- Citation and provenance notes
- New observation records
- Update recommendations

## Required Questions

- What audience is this for?
- Which canonical objects does this rely on?
- What claims require citation?
- What new observations might this publication create?
- Which outputs should update when upstream knowledge changes?

## Allowed Work

- Translate canonical knowledge into audience-specific communication.
- Preserve uncertainty where it exists.
- Maintain citations and provenance.
- Feed publication outcomes back into observation.

## Prohibited Work

- Do not publish unsupported claims.
- Do not turn rhetoric into canonical knowledge.
- Do not expose proprietary, instructor-only, or assessment-secure content.
- Do not treat outputs as final truth.

## Exit Criteria

The publication is traceable, audience-appropriate, and ready to become a new observation in the lifecycle.
