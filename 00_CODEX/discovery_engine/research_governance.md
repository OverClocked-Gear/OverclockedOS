# Research Governance

## Purpose

Research governance defines how knowledge moves through the Discovery Engine without premature certainty.

The CODEX shall distinguish between observation, hypothesis, evidence, concept, framework, and canonical knowledge.

## Knowledge States

### Observation

A recorded signal from reality. It may describe a source, event, behavior, metric, interview, document, or publication. Observation does not imply truth.

### Hypothesis

A provisional, testable explanation generated from observations. No hypothesis shall automatically become a concept.

### Evidence

Information used to support, weaken, contradict, or contextualize a hypothesis, concept, relationship, or canonical object.

### Concept

A validated explanatory idea with reusable value. A concept should explain behavior, adaptation, resistance, learning, trust, coordination, or change.

### Framework

A structured model that organizes concepts and relationships for analysis or use. No concept shall automatically become a framework.

### Canonical Knowledge

Accepted, evidence-backed knowledge maintained in the Adaptive Intelligence Layer, Kinetic Stack, or other canonical ontology locations. No framework shall automatically become canonical.

## Statuses

- Observation
- Hypothesis
- Emerging
- Validated
- Canonical
- Rejected

## Governance Rules

- Evidence determines progression.
- Hypotheses remain provisional until validated.
- Concepts require explanatory value.
- Frameworks require structure, boundaries, and evidence.
- Canonical knowledge requires duplicate review and ontology fit.
- Contradictory evidence must be preserved.
- Confidence must be explicit.
- Research gaps should produce research agendas.

## Research Agenda Generation

Whenever evidence clusters emerge, the CODEX shall generate:

- Research opportunities
- Research questions
- Recommended literature
- Potential methodologies
- Suggested interviews
- Suggested datasets
- Potential surveys
- Potential experiments
- Potential case studies

The objective is to guide future research rather than merely summarize existing research.

## Review Requirements

Before promoting knowledge to a stronger state, review:

- Evidence strength
- Contradictory evidence
- Methodological fit
- Duplicate risk
- Canonical object fit
- Application dependencies
- Publication risk

## Default Bias

When uncertainty remains, recommend additional research rather than canonical promotion.
