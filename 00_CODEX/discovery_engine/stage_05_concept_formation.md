# Stage 05: Concept Formation

## Purpose

Only validated hypotheses become concepts.

Concept formation converts validated, explanatory hypotheses into reusable Adaptive Intelligence concepts. Concepts should never be created solely because multiple papers mention similar language.

## Concept Standard

A concept must demonstrate explanatory value. It should help explain why humans, teams, organizations, ecosystems, or institutions behave, adapt, resist, learn, coordinate, or change.

## Outputs

- Concept objects
- Evidence maps
- Research status
- Concept boundary notes
- Canonical candidate recommendations

## Required Questions

- What validated hypothesis supports this concept?
- What does this concept explain?
- What evidence defines its boundaries?
- What did Devil's Advocate review challenge?
- What did Skeptic review determine about novelty or duplicate risk?
- What similar concepts already exist?
- Should an existing concept be strengthened instead?

## Allowed Work

- Promote validated hypotheses into concept candidates.
- Map evidence to concept claims.
- Define scope and boundaries.
- Incorporate Devil's Advocate and Skeptic review findings.
- Compare with existing Adaptive Intelligence concepts.
- Recommend canonical inclusion.

## Prohibited Work

- Do not create concepts from language similarity alone.
- Do not duplicate existing concepts.
- Do not create frameworks automatically.
- Do not promote hypotheses that have not passed validation and adversarial review.
- Do not treat concepts as canonical until accepted through ontology enrichment.

## Exit Criteria

The concept candidate has explanatory value, evidence support, clear boundaries, and a recommendation for ontology enrichment or further research.
