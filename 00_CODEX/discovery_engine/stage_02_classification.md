# Stage 02: Classification

## Purpose

Describe observations using canonical taxonomy.

Classification determines what an observation appears to discuss. It organizes evidence for research without validating claims or creating canonical concepts.

## Outputs

- Domains
- Capabilities
- Industries
- Technologies
- Business models
- Operating models
- Confidence
- Review status

## Required Questions

- What does this discuss?
- Which capabilities appear?
- Which domains are affected?
- Which industries, technologies, business models, or operating models are implicated?
- How confident is the classification?

## Allowed Work

- Apply explicit classification methods.
- Use canonical taxonomy labels when available.
- Record confidence and uncertainty.
- Mark weak or ambiguous classifications for review.
- Prepare clusters for hypothesis generation.

## Prohibited Work

- Do not present classifications as conclusions.
- Do not infer concepts without validation.
- Do not duplicate canonical taxonomy objects.
- Do not modify evidence.

## Exit Criteria

The observation has enough descriptive structure to be compared with other observations and used for provisional hypothesis generation.
