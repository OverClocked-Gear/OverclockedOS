# Stage 02: Classification

## Purpose

Determine what evidence discusses.

Classification assigns evidence to useful topic, domain, publisher, source type, and relevance categories. This stage describes the subject matter of evidence without claiming that any concept is canonical.

## Classification Examples

- Industry
- Technology
- Capabilities
- Business models
- Operating models
- Customer experience
- Frameworks
- Output relevance

## Allowed Work

- Classify evidence using explicit classification methods.
- Record confidence and review status.
- Use filename-, metadata-, abstract-, or content-based methods when authorized.
- Tag evidence for downstream concept extraction.
- Mark ambiguous or weak signals for review.

## Prohibited Work

- Do not modify evidence.
- Do not declare concepts canonical.
- Do not duplicate existing canonical objects.
- Do not generate applications.
- Do not invent publisher, date, citation, or policy facts.

## Outputs

- Evidence Classification Manifest
- Classification summary
- Review queue
- Method notes

## Compiler Questions

- What evidence changed?
- What classification became stronger?
- What classifications remain uncertain?
- Which evidence should move to concept extraction?

## Exit Criteria

Evidence has enough topical structure to support concept candidate extraction and research gap identification.
